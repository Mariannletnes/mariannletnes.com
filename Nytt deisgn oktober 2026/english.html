About

Digital technology, Bildung and pedagogical practice

I am an Associate Professor of Education at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology, where I work at the intersection of digital technology, Bildung and pedagogical practice.

My doctoral thesis, Digital Bildung in Early Childhood Education (2014), examined how young children make meaning through the production of multimodal media, and how such work can contribute to their digital Bildung. That question has stayed with me, and has since expanded across the educational lifespan: from kindergarten to school, teacher education and higher education.

A recurring theme in my work is that digital competence is rarely developed by individuals attending courses. It develops collectively, within professional communities, in organisations that have committed to something and sustained it over several years. This is why my research and my development work belong together rather than running in parallel.

Research interests

  • Professional digital competence. How teachers, teacher educators and school leaders develop digital competence collectively, and what organisational conditions make it last.
  • Bildung and digital technology. Digital technology as a question of formation rather than of tools, drawing on Klafki's concept of categorial Bildung.
  • Aesthetic learning processes. Multimodality, sensory knowing and creative work as routes into learning, in early childhood education and in schools.
  • Generative AI in education. What AI does to knowledge production, assessment practices and the epistemic infrastructure of classrooms.
  • Educational leadership. The role of leaders in building sustained professional learning communities.

DIGIT

Digitalisation, learning and assessment in primary and secondary education, with five work packages and sixteen researchers from NTNU, the University of Stavanger and SINTEF. Research strand · AI-Learn

Artificial intelligence in pupils' learning processes, in instructional design and in assessment practices. Design-based research carried out with teachers and pupils in authentic classrooms.
